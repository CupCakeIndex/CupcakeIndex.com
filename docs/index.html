How it works

Every Monday morning the model downloads the weekend's results and rebuilds the rankings from scratch. Each team gets a set of scores from 0 to 100, where 50 is average. The sliders decide how much each score counts.

    The cupcake problem (college)

    Every FCS opponent is rated like a team about 25 points worse than an average FBS team, and FCS games count only 25% as much in the power rating. Margins are capped at 24 points. Beating an FCS team 70-0 proves about as much as a close loss to an average FBS team. On top of that, the Cupcake score measures how padded a schedule is relative to the team playing it. An opponent counts as a cupcake once they are below average and at least 14 points worse than you on a neutral field, and bigger mismatches count more. FCS opponents always count. A bad team playing other bad teams isn't padding; a top-10 team scheduling them is. A higher Cupcake score counts against a team. It only counts games already played: a cupcake still on the schedule is added once the game is final.

    Schedule vs. Cupcake

    Schedule asks "how hard was your road?" It's the average strength of everyone you played, and it's the same no matter how good you are. Cupcake asks "how much did you pad it?" It only looks at the games far below your level. Together they give every team a schedule profile:

    • GAUNTLET hard schedule, no fluff.
    • BARBELL hard schedule, but padded with cupcakes too.
    • GRIND easier schedule, but no padding. They played their peers.
    • CUPCAKE WALK easy schedule and padded. The records to be most skeptical of.

    NFL

    NFL rankings use the same engine. Résumé is measured against a top-8 team, efficiency is opponent-adjusted EPA per play, and margins are capped at 21 points. Each team's panel shows the starting QB for every game and flags when someone other than the usual starter played or is listed to start. The rating itself doesn't know about QB changes, so a ⚠ is a good reason to trust it less. Monday's update runs before Monday Night Football. Tuesday's update adds that game.

    NFL cupcakes. Nobody in the NFL schedules a cupcake, but the schedule still hands some teams more of them. The NFL Cupcake score uses the same rule as college, scaled to NFL ratings: an opponent counts once they're in roughly the bottom third of the league (3+ points worse than an average team) and at least 6 points worse than you, and counts fully at 14+ points worse. Two bad teams playing each other isn't padding. NFL teams get the same schedule profiles, and the Cupcake Bully of the Week is the biggest beating (17+ points) of a cupcake that week.

    Early season

    In the first few weeks there isn't enough data, so ratings start from a preseason guess: last year's rating scaled by returning production, plus roster talent. That guess fades to zero within a few weeks. After that it's pure on-field results.

    Keeping it honest

    Each week the ratings pick every game of the following week and set their own point spread. The Picks tab compares that spread with sportsbook lines and tracks how often the model is right, both straight up and against the spread.

    Scores, stats and standings

    Live scores, box scores, player stats, rosters and standings load straight from ESPN's public data feed in your browser. They update automatically every 30 seconds while games are in progress.
