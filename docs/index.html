Privacy

You don't need an account to use the Cupcake Index. Without one, your Pick'em picks, daily game and fantasy team stay in this browser only, and we never see them.

If you sign in

  • Sign-in is handled by Google. We never see or store your password.
  • What we keep: an account ID, your first name, and your game data (Pick'em picks and record, daily game progress and stats, fantasy team), so it shows up on every device you sign in on.
  • What we don't keep: no email list, no birthday, no location, no payment info. We don't sell or share anything, and there are no ads.
  • Where it lives: Google Firebase. Security rules let each account read and change only its own data.
  • Delete it any time: Settings (the gear) > Account > Delete my account removes everything we stored for you.

How it works

Every Monday morning the model downloads the weekend's results and rebuilds the rankings from scratch. Each team gets a set of scores from 0 to 100, where 50 is average. The sliders decide how much each score counts.

    The cupcake problem (college)

    Every FCS opponent is rated like a team about 25 points worse than an average FBS team, and FCS games count only 25% as much in the power rating. Margins are capped at 24 points. Beating an FCS team 70-0 proves about as much as a close loss to an average FBS team. On top of that, the Cupcake score measures how padded a schedule is relative to the team playing it. An opponent counts as a cupcake once they are below average and at least 14 points worse than you on a neutral field, and bigger mismatches count more. FCS opponents always count. A bad team playing other bad teams isn't padding; a top-10 team scheduling them is. A higher Cupcake score counts against a team. It only counts games already played: a cupcake still on the schedule is added once the game is final.

    Schedule vs. Cupcake

    Schedule asks "how hard was your road?" It's the average strength of everyone you played, and it's the same no matter how good you are. Cupcake asks "how much did you pad it?" It only looks at the games far below your level. Together they give every team a schedule profile:

    • GAUNTLET hard schedule, no fluff.
    • BARBELL hard schedule, but padded with cupcakes too.
    • GRIND easier schedule, but no padding. They played their peers.
    • CUPCAKE WALK easy schedule and padded. The records to be most skeptical of.

    NFL

    The NFL has its own rating model, built for a 17-game league where most games are close. Four differences from college: (1) last season's rating carries over all year instead of fading out after a few weeks, because NFL rosters carry over; (2) the rating blends scoring margin with opponent-adjusted EPA per play (how well a team moves the ball and stops it, play by play), which predicts next week better than scores alone; (3) recent weeks count a bit more than September; (4) a team starting someone other than its usual quarterback is marked down. Margins are capped at 17 points and home field is worth 1 point. We tested it on every game from 2020 to 2025 using only what was known before kickoff: it picked 64.4% of winners (the old settings: 62.6%; the sportsbooks' favorite: 66.8%). Résumé is measured against a top-8 team. Monday's update runs before Monday Night Football. Tuesday's update adds that game.

    NFL cupcakes. Nobody in the NFL schedules a cupcake, and any given Sunday is real, so only a true mismatch counts: the opponent has to be clearly bad (about the bottom quarter of the league) and at least 5 points worse than you, with full credit at 10+ points worse. A good team like the Lions is never anyone's cupcake. NFL teams get the same schedule profiles. The Cupcake Bully of the Week is the most lopsided win (14+ points) over a weaker team, scored on the margin plus how big the mismatch was.

    Early season

    In the first few weeks there isn't enough data, so ratings start from a preseason guess: last year's rating scaled by returning production, plus roster talent. That guess fades to zero within a few weeks. After that it's pure on-field results.

    Keeping it honest

    Each week the ratings pick every game of the following week. On the Pick'em page you make your own picks for the week's games, with the model's win chance shown as a small hint, and see how you did once the games are final.

    Scores, stats and standings

    Live scores, box scores, player stats, rosters and standings load straight from ESPN's public data feed in your browser. They update automatically every 30 seconds while games are in progress.
